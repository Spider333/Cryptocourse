A complete, university-grade course for Computer Science students on how Bitcoin, Ethereum, cryptocurrencies and blockchain technology work in depth. Taught at the [Faculty of Informatics and Information Technologies](https://www.fiit.stuba.sk/) of the [Slovak University of Technology](https://www.stuba.sk/) by [David Stancel, MSc.](https://davidstancel.com) of [CoinStory](https://coinstory.tech/) and Kristian Kostal, PhD.

The creation of this course has been supported by Ernst & Young. Thank you!

> **2027 edition.** Rebuilt in November 2026 around seminal papers, which age far slower than blog posts. The [2021 edition](https://github.com/Spider333/Cryptocourse/tree/108171a3e1a62a6bedadff6787941727b4141bbc), with its original reading lists, stays available.

> **Free to use.** Use anything here for any purpose, as long as you credit the source. Comments, questions, topic ideas and resources to include are welcome at **david@coinstory.tech**.

## Part I: Foundations

### 1. Money, Cypherpunks and the Road to Bitcoin

**Topics**

- What money is, and why digital cash is hard: double spending and trusted third parties
- Attempts before Bitcoin: DigiCash, e-gold, Hashcash, b-money, Bit Gold, RPOW
- The building blocks Bitcoin combined: linked timestamping, proof-of-work, digital signatures and Byzantine agreement
- The emergence of Bitcoin, main principles and definitions

**Core reading**

- [Bitcoin's Academic Pedigree](https://doi.org/10.1145/3132259) by A. Narayanan, J. Clark (2017)
- [Bitcoin: A Peer-to-Peer Electronic Cash System](https://bitcoin.org/bitcoin.pdf) by S. Nakamoto (2008)
- [CoinStory](https://coinstory.tech/) by D. Stancel, as a companion on how cryptocurrencies evolved

### 2. Cryptography for Blockchains

**Topics**

- Hash functions and their security properties, Merkle trees
- Public-key cryptography on elliptic curves: secp256k1, ECDSA and Schnorr signatures
- Commitments, and why privacy coins and layer 2s depend on them
- Keys, seed phrases and hierarchical deterministic wallets (BIP 32, BIP 39)
- Addresses and encodings: Base58Check, Bech32 and Bech32m

**Core reading**

- [A Graduate Course in Applied Cryptography](https://toc.cryptobook.us/) by D. Boneh, V. Shoup: the chapters on collision-resistant hashing and digital signatures
- [Mastering Bitcoin](https://github.com/bitcoinbook/bitcoinbook), chapters 4 (keys) and 5 (wallets)
- [BIP 340: Schnorr Signatures for secp256k1](https://github.com/bitcoin/bips/blob/master/bip-0340.mediawiki)

### 4. Consensus: Nakamoto, BFT and Proof-of-Stake

**Topics**

- The Byzantine generals problem and the FLP impossibility result
- Classical Byzantine fault tolerance: PBFT, Tendermint, HotStuff
- Nakamoto consensus: mining, difficulty adjustment, the heaviest chain rule, and its formal security analysis
- Proof-of-stake: Ouroboros, Casper FFG and Gasper, finality and slashing
- Trade-offs: safety versus liveness, probabilistic versus economic finality, energy use

**Core reading**

- [The Bitcoin Backbone Protocol: Analysis and Applications](https://eprint.iacr.org/2014/765) by J. Garay, A. Kiayias, N. Leonardos (2015)
- [Practical Byzantine Fault Tolerance](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/practical-byzantine-fault-tolerance/) by M. Castro, B. Liskov (1999)
- [Combining GHOST and Casper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.03052) by V. Buterin et al. (2020), the consensus Ethereum runs today

<details>

## Part IV: Privacy, security and law

### 12. Privacy: Zcash, Monero and Zero-Knowledge Proofs

**Topics**

- How Bitcoin payments get traced: address clustering, change detection, network-level deanonymisation
- Zcash: from Zerocash to Halo 2 and the Orchard pool, shielded addresses, no trusted setup
- Monero: CryptoNote, stealth addresses, ring signatures, RingCT, CLSAG, Bulletproofs+, and the move to full-chain membership proofs (FCMP++)
- Zero-knowledge proofs: SNARKs versus STARKs, Groth16, PLONK
